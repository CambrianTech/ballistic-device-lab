BALLISTIC GEOMETRY SCREEN -- exploratory simulation, 2026-10-22

Scope
Classical ray tracing in a square four-terminal cavity, with an empty junction,
triangular excluded region, or wall-attached wedges. This is a geometry and
collimation-sensitivity screen, not a prediction of graphene rectification,
conversion efficiency, receiver sensitivity, or a patentable new device.

Reference
Auton et al., Graphene ballistic nano-rectifier with very high responsivity,
Nature Communications 7,11670 (2016), doi:10.1038/ncomms11670.
Author manuscript:
https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/33718005/CLEAN_Main_Text_Graphene_ballistic.pdf
The article attributes the nonlinear effect to current-induced collimation
changing transmission probabilities (page 4). Its experimental performance is
not used to calibrate this model. Our geometries are illustrative, not replicas.

Model and normalization
Square side 2 normalized units; four equal centered contact openings width .6.
Specular ray reflection off impenetrable polygon boundaries. Injection positions
are uniform across each contact. Equilibrium flux uses theta=asin(U[-1,1]),
which samples cosine angular flux rather than uniform angle. Imposed collimation
uses theta=asin(U[-.35,.35]), restricting incidence to about +/-20.5 degrees.
Both injected populations have the same number of rays. The missing relation
between current/voltage and this angular distribution is NOT assumed.
Diffuse wall scattering samples a cosine law into the accessible half plane.
All rays must exit; unresolved trajectories raise an error rather than being
discarded. A high reflection cap accommodates rare grazing trajectories.
No bulk scattering, electrostatic forces, graphene band structure, quantum
interference, phonons, contact resistance or finite frequency response included.
Room temperature is not an input to this scale-free geometric calculation.

Score
S = mean over L/R injection of [P(exit Bottom)-P(exit Top)].
Objective = S(collimated)-S(equilibrium angular distribution).
This is a probability difference, NOT a percent efficiency or net equilibrium
current. Reservoirs at the output terminals also inject carriers; one-sided
steering does not imply spontaneous current.

Search and validation
36 triangles: width .3,.6,.9,1.2; height .3,.6,.9; vertical offset -.25,0,.25.
4096 rays per input per angular condition; same seeds across candidates.
Maximize signed positive objective; a mirrored geometry supplies opposite sign.
This coarse grid does not establish a global optimum (winner reaches width bound).
Freeze winner then validate with 32768 rays/input/condition, four new seeds,
at specularity 1,.8,.5,0. Error bars are seed standard deviations, not confidence
intervals or fabrication uncertainty. No selection using holdout trials.
Baseline reciprocal matrices: 32768 rays/contact, cosine input all four contacts.
Raw reciprocity residuals are reported. Linear electrical diagnostic averages
each matrix with its transpose, removes diagonal reflections and forms the
conductance graph Laplacian. Solve floating top/bottom voltages for L/R +/-0.5.
Reverse drive gives negative output, so average DC is zero by linearity. This
is a deliberate sanity check, not a simulated nonlinear rectification result.
Additional checks: straight-junction symmetry and sign reversal under vertical
reflection. Probability conservation follows from every ray exiting once.

Interpretation
The selected triangle changes directional steering by about 24 percentage
points under the imposed collimation change. This survives sampling variation
and is reduced by diffuse boundary scattering. It establishes geometric
sensitivity within this billiard model only. A useful next electrical model
must solve bias-dependent injection/collimation and self-consistent potentials,
then measure loaded DC output and input power. Do not infer watts, responsivity,
bandwidth, or advantage over existing rectifiers from this result.
